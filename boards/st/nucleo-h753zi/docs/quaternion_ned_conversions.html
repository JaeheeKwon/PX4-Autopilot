Quaternion, Euler, and NED Conversion Derivations

This page derives the attitude conversion equations used by the EKF2 notes: ZYX roll-pitch-yaw to quaternion, quaternion back to Euler angles, and quaternion rotation from aircraft body frame to local NED navigation frame.

Convention: ZYX yaw-pitch-roll Quaternion: [w, x, y, z] Frames: body to NED

Index

1. Conventions 2. Euler to Quaternion 3. Quaternion to Euler 4. Body to NED 5. EKF2 Usage 6. Links

1. Conventions

A quaternion is written as \(q=[w,x,y,z]\), where \(w\) is the scalar part and \([x,y,z]\) is the vector part. This document uses Hamilton quaternion multiplication:

\[ q \otimes r = \begin{bmatrix} w_q w_r - v_q \cdot v_r\\ w_q v_r + w_r v_q + v_q \times v_r \end{bmatrix} \]

For a unit quaternion, the inverse is the conjugate:

\[ q^{-1}=q^*=[w,-x,-y,-z] \]

The aircraft body frame has \(x_b\) forward, \(y_b\) right, and \(z_b\) down. The NED navigation frame has \(x_n\) north, \(y_n\) east, and \(z_n\) down. Roll is \(\phi\), pitch is \(\theta\), and yaw is \(\psi\).

2. Euler to Quaternion

For the aerospace ZYX convention, the combined rotation is yaw about NED down, then pitch, then roll:

\[ q = q_z(\psi)\otimes q_y(\theta)\otimes q_x(\phi) \]

The three single-axis rotation quaternions are:

\[ q_x(\phi)= \begin{bmatrix} c_\phi\\s_\phi\\0\\0 \end{bmatrix},\quad q_y(\theta)= \begin{bmatrix} c_\theta\\0\\s_\theta\\0 \end{bmatrix},\quad q_z(\psi)= \begin{bmatrix} c_\psi\\0\\0\\s_\psi \end{bmatrix} \] \[ c_\phi=\cos\frac{\phi}{2},\quad s_\phi=\sin\frac{\phi}{2},\quad c_\theta=\cos\frac{\theta}{2},\quad s_\theta=\sin\frac{\theta}{2},\quad c_\psi=\cos\frac{\psi}{2},\quad s_\psi=\sin\frac{\psi}{2} \]

First multiply yaw and pitch:

\[ q_z\otimes q_y = \begin{bmatrix} c_\psi c_\theta\\ -s_\psi s_\theta\\ c_\psi s_\theta\\ s_\psi c_\theta \end{bmatrix} \]

Then multiply the result by the roll quaternion:

\[ q=(q_z\otimes q_y)\otimes q_x = \begin{bmatrix} w\\x\\y\\z \end{bmatrix} \] \[ \begin{aligned} w &= c_\phi c_\theta c_\psi + s_\phi s_\theta s_\psi\\ x &= s_\phi c_\theta c_\psi - c_\phi s_\theta s_\psi\\ y &= c_\phi s_\theta c_\psi + s_\phi c_\theta s_\psi\\ z &= c_\phi c_\theta s_\psi - s_\phi s_\theta c_\psi \end{aligned} \]

3. Quaternion to Euler

To derive the inverse equations, compare the rotation matrix from a quaternion to the ZYX Euler rotation matrix.

\[ R(q)= \begin{bmatrix} 1 - 2(y^2 + z^2) & 2(xy - wz) & 2(xz + wy)\\ 2(xy + wz) & 1 - 2(x^2 + z^2) & 2(yz - wx)\\ 2(xz - wy) & 2(yz + wx) & 1 - 2(x^2 + y^2) \end{bmatrix} \]
\[ R_{ZYX}(\phi,\theta,\psi)= \begin{bmatrix} c_\psi c_\theta & c_\psi s_\theta s_\phi - s_\psi c_\phi & c_\psi s_\theta c_\phi + s_\psi s_\phi\\ s_\psi c_\theta & s_\psi s_\theta s_\phi + c_\psi c_\phi & s_\psi s_\theta c_\phi - c_\psi s_\phi\\ -s_\theta & c_\theta s_\phi & c_\theta c_\phi \end{bmatrix} \]

The pitch equation comes from the third row, first column:

\[ R_{31}=-\sin\theta=2(xz-wy) \] \[ \theta=\arcsin(2(wy-zx)) \]

Roll comes from \(R_{32}=c_\theta s_\phi\) and \(R_{33}=c_\theta c_\phi\):

\[ \phi=\operatorname{atan2}(R_{32},R_{33}) \] \[ \phi = \operatorname{atan2}\left(2(w x + y z),\ 1 - 2(x^2 + y^2)\right) \]

Yaw comes from \(R_{21}=s_\psi c_\theta\) and \(R_{11}=c_\psi c_\theta\):

\[ \psi=\operatorname{atan2}(R_{21},R_{11}) \] \[ \psi = \operatorname{atan2}\left(2(w z + x y),\ 1 - 2(y^2 + z^2)\right) \]
Clamp the argument to asin() into [-1, 1] in software. A unit quaternion can still produce a value slightly outside that range because of floating-point roundoff.

4. Body Frame to NED Frame

A vector is rotated by treating it as a pure quaternion, applying the attitude quaternion, and then dropping the scalar component:

\[ \bar v^b=[0,v_x,v_y,v_z] \] \[ \bar v^n = q\otimes \bar v^b \otimes q^* \]

Expanding this multiplication for \(q=[w,x,y,z]\) gives the body-to-NED matrix:

\[ v^n = R^n_b(q)v^b \] \[ R^n_b = \begin{bmatrix} 1 - 2(y^2 + z^2) & 2(xy - wz) & 2(xz + wy)\\ 2(xy + wz) & 1 - 2(x^2 + z^2) & 2(yz - wx)\\ 2(xz - wy) & 2(yz + wx) & 1 - 2(x^2 + y^2) \end{bmatrix} \]

Therefore:

\[ \begin{bmatrix} v_N\\v_E\\v_D \end{bmatrix} = R^n_b(q) \begin{bmatrix} v_x\\v_y\\v_z \end{bmatrix}_b \]

A free vector only needs rotation. A point attached to the body needs rotation plus translation by the vehicle position in NED:

\[ p^n = p^n_{\text{vehicle}} + R^n_b(q)p^b \]

5. EKF2 Usage

EKF2 stores attitude as quat_nominal. The C++ code converts that quaternion into _R_to_earth, then rotates corrected IMU delta velocity from body frame into earth/NED frame: 

_R_to_earth = Dcmf(_state.quat_nominal);
const Vector3f corrected_delta_vel_ef = _R_to_earth * corrected_delta_vel;

This is a vector rotation, so no translation is applied. Position corrections are a separate state update.
